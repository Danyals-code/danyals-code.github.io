Tools

Built to answer questions faster.

Spatial apps, design tools and research instruments. Most of them started because a study needed something that didn’t exist yet, and the code is on GitHub.

iOS · visionOS · Swift

Lemmy AR Experience

A native app that anchors a life-size, animated robot to your floor so you can judge its motion in your own space. I built it for the AR stage of my thesis and distributed it to study participants through TestFlight.

It’s written in Swift with SwiftUI, ARKit and RealityKit. It has 20 animations grouped as base, functional and expressive, plane detection and placement, pinch to pause, and screenshots without the interface. Settings include high contrast, text size, haptics and sound.

Web app · React Three Fiber

AR/VR UI Designer

A drag-and-drop designer for visionOS-style interfaces. Lay out windows, panels and controls on a 3D canvas, then preview them at eye level with HDRI lighting and real glass blur. It also supports volumes with 3D content, and you don’t need Xcode, a Mac or a headset.

Blender add-on · Python · GPL-3.0

True RoboAnimator

Turns keyframed chassis animation into physically plausible motion for two-wheeled robots. It flags sideways slip, repairs impossible paths, computes wheel angles and RPM within speed limits, and exports CSV or JSON for engineers and AR apps.

  • 7,000+ downloads
  • Blender 4.2+
  • Official Blender Extensions

Web experiment · JavaScript

Head-tracked perspective

Fish-tank VR in the browser. Webcam face tracking drives off-axis perspective, so your monitor becomes a window onto a small 3D room. It builds on classic work by Ware, Arthur and Booth, and by Deering. No video leaves the device.

Live demo GitHub
Blender add-on · Python

MultiView Product Renderer

Turns CAD products (STEP or STL) into consistent image sets. It centers each model, rigs orthographic and perspective cameras with three-point lighting, and batch-renders every view with predictable file names. Useful for AI training data and product form analysis.
