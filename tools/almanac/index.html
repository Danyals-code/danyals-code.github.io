Almanac

Every day, as the ancients counted it.

An iPhone app that names any day from 10,000 BC to AD 3000 the way the Maya, the Egyptians and the astronomers of China and Islam counted it, and shows when sixteen ancient sites line up with the sun, the moon and the stars.

Visit the Almanac site Get the highlights

Coming soon to the App Store.

Ancient calendars and the sky they were built to follow. Almanac names any day in four of them, calculated on the iPhone from published astronomy, and shows the moments when old stones still line up with the sun.

Get the highlights.

  • Turn the wheels of time. Spin the Maya, Egyptian, Islamic and Chinese calendar wheels with a finger and watch the days go round.

  • Where stone meets sky. Drag through the hours and watch the sun cross each plan into line, on a compass that turns with the iPhone.

  • Every eclipse, on the moon wheel. Solar and lunar eclipses, with what you can see of each from where you are.

  • Today, as the ancients counted it. One date, written the way four civilizations would have written it.

  • Your birthday, anciently. Your Maya day sign, Egyptian season and Chinese animal, with Hijri, Hebrew and Indian dates, shared as a card.

  • Reminders and widgets. Follow a site for a reminder before its next alignment, and keep a wheel, a site or a sky compass on the Home Screen.

Made to be explored.

Everything the app does, at a glance.

  • Four calendars. Maya, Egyptian, Islamic and Chinese. Type a date into any of them, from 13.0.0.0.0 to 1 Thoth or 1 Muharram.

  • Sixteen ancient sites. Stonehenge, Newgrange, Giza, Chichén Itzá and more, from Göbekli Tepe to Machu Picchu.

  • A compass that turns with you. Each site’s plan sits on a compass that turns with the iPhone.

  • The sky above you. Where the sun rises and sets for you at the solstices and equinoxes.

  • Every eclipse. Solar and lunar, with what you can see of each from where you are.

  • Your birthday, anciently. Day sign, season and animal, plus Hijri, Hebrew and Indian dates, to share as a card.

  • Reminders. Follow a site to be reminded before its next alignment.

  • Widgets. A calendar wheel, an ancient site or a sky compass on the Home Screen.

  • Shown with its sources. Tap “Show the math” on any alignment to see where it comes from, starting with Jean Meeus’s Astronomical Algorithms.

  • Private by design. Everything is calculated on the iPhone and works offline. No account, ads, analytics or tracking.

  • Works offline. No connection needed to turn a wheel, find an eclipse or check an alignment.

  • Thirteen thousand years. From 10,000 BC to AD 3000, in every calendar.
