Human-centered AI

How do people read what AI makes?

Perception study · IASDR 2025

Can people still tell what’s real?

A controlled online study: 87 people, 36 clips, three generation techniques and three themes. Accuracy was 60%, and context mattered more than the model.

Interaction experiments.

Small studies and prototypes on classic HCI questions: pointing, window management and head-coupled perspective. The first two began in UNIST’s Advanced Human–Computer Interaction course in 2025.

Fitts’ law, three ways

Throughput in bits per second. Higher is better.

  • Mouse5.6
  • Wacom Cintiq Prostylus4.0
  • iPad with mouse2.2

WebFitts tasks at three amplitudes (200, 400, 800 px) and three widths (40, 80, 160 px), 11 targets per condition, one session per device.

Pointing performance

The mouse won, and iPadOS pointer support lost.

I compared a mouse, a Wacom Cintiq Pro stylus and an iPad driven by a mouse. The mouse was fastest and most accurate. The stylus suffered from misclicks and calibration offsets. The iPad was slowest, with very little pointer control inside apps.

Interaction technique

Windows that slide and snap.

A window-management prototype built with Unreal Engine Blueprints. Windows move with velocity and a speed limit, check for collisions, and snap to screen edges and corners, with tunable snap strength and distances.

Head-coupled perspective

A monitor that works like a window.

Fish-tank VR in the browser. Webcam face tracking drives an off-axis projection, so the screen becomes a window onto a small 3D room that shifts as you move your head. It builds on classic work by Ware, Arthur and Booth, and by Deering. No video leaves the device.
